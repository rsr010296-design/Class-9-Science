Intro

Why improve food production?

Food gives us proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins and minerals. We get most of it from agriculture and animal husbandry.

Green Revolution

Increased food-grain production.

White Revolution

Better and more efficient use and availability of milk.

Sustainable practices

Grow more food without degrading the environment.

Food security depends on

Availability + Access

Storing grain in warehouses is not enough. People need money to buy food, so farmers' incomes must rise.

For sustained livelihood

Mixed & integrated farming

Combine agriculture with livestock, poultry, fisheries and bee-keeping. Also use intercropping.

Find a variety that gives a good yield. Desirable characters are added by hybridisation (crossing genetically dissimilar plants) or by adding a gene, which gives genetically modified (GM) crops.

15.1.2

Crop production management: nutrients

Production practices depend on the farmer's money: no cost, low cost and high cost. Higher inputs usually give higher yields. Plants need 16 essential nutrients from air, water and soil.

AirWaterSoil · macronutrient (6)Soil · micronutrient (7)
Tap any nutrient to see where it comes from.

Macro-nutrients

Needed in large quantities: N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S.

Micro-nutrients

Needed in small quantities: Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl.

Trick: Air gives C and O. Water gives H (and O). Soil gives the other 13. Deficiency affects reproduction, growth and makes plants more likely to get diseases.

Types of manure

Type (i)

Compost

Farm waste (cow dung, vegetable waste, straw, weeds, sewage) decomposed in pits.

Type (i)

Vermi-compost

Compost made using earthworms to speed up decomposition.

Type (ii)

Green manure

Plants like sun hemp or guar are grown and ploughed into the soil. Adds nitrogen and phosphorus.

Organic farming

Minimal or no chemicals. Uses organic manures, recycled farm waste, bio-agents like blue-green algae (biofertilizer), neem leaves or turmeric as bio-pesticides in grain storage, plus mixed cropping, intercropping and crop rotation.

15.1.2

Irrigation

Most Indian farming is rain-fed, so a poor monsoon means crop failure. Giving water at the right stages raises yield.

Wells

  • Dug wells: water from water-bearing strata.
  • Tube wells: tap deeper strata.
  • Water lifted by pumps.

Canals

Elaborate system. Water from reservoirs or rivers flows: main canal → branch canals → distributaries → fields.

River lift systems

Water drawn directly from rivers where canal flow is low. Used in areas close to rivers.

Tanks

Small storage reservoirs that store run-off from small catchment areas.

New ideas: Rainwater harvesting and watershed management. Small check-dams raise ground water levels, stop rainwater flowing away and reduce soil erosion. Light soils hold less water, so crops there suffer more in droughts.

15.1.3

Crop protection & storage

Weeds, insect pests and diseases can destroy most of a crop if not controlled in time.

Weeds

Unwanted plants that compete for food, space and light.

  • Xanthium (gokhroo)
  • Parthenium (gajar ghas)
  • Cyperinus rotundus (motha)

Insect pests attack in 3 ways

  • Cut root, stem and leaf
  • Suck cell sap
  • Bore into stem and fruits

Diseases

Caused by bacteria, fungi and viruses, spread through soil, water and air.

Control methods

  • Pesticides: herbicides, insecticides, fungicides. Overuse is poisonous and pollutes.
  • Mechanical removal of weeds.
  • Preventive: proper seed bed, timely sowing, intercropping, crop rotation, resistant varieties, summer ploughing (deep ploughing in summer kills weeds and pests).

Storage of grains

Biotic causes: insects, rodents, fungi, mites, bacteria.
Abiotic causes: wrong moisture and temperature.

Effects: poor quality, weight loss, poor germination, discolouration.

Prevention: clean produce, dry it first in sun then in shade, and fumigate.

15.2.1 Cattle farming

Two species, two purposes

Bos indicus = cows · Bos bubalis = buffaloes

  • Milch animals: milk-producing females (dairy).
  • Draught animals: farm labour like tilling, irrigation, carting.

Cross-breeding

Exotic (Jersey, Brown Swiss) → long lactation period
Local (Red Sindhi, Sahiwal) → disease resistance

Cross them to get both qualities.

Food & shelter

  • Food: maintenance requirement + milk-producing requirement.
  • Feed: roughage (mostly fibre) + concentrates (low fibre, high protein).
  • Well-ventilated roofed shed, sloping floor, regular brushing.

Diseases

  • External parasites live on skin, cause skin disease.
  • Internal parasites: worms (stomach, intestine), flukes (liver).
  • Bacteria and viruses → vaccination.

15.2.2 Poultry farming

Layers vs Broilers

Layers → eggs
Broilers → meat. Fed vitamin-rich, protein-rich feed with adequate fat; high vitamins A and K.

Cross-breeding

Indian Aseel × foreign Leghorn, for:

  • More and better chicks
  • Dwarf broiler parent
  • Tolerance to high temperature
  • Low maintenance
  • Smaller layer that eats cheap fibrous diet
Common in dairy and poultry: clean shelter, balanced feed, hygiene, disinfectants and vaccination.
15.2.3

Fish production

Fish is a cheap source of animal protein. It includes finned true fish and shellfish like prawns and molluscs.

From nature

Capture fishing

Catching fish from natural sources. Marine fish (pomphret, mackerel, tuna, sardines, Bombay duck) are located using satellites and echo-sounders. India has 7500 km of coastline.

Farming in sea

Mariculture

Culture of marine fish: mullets, bhetki, pearl spots, prawns, mussels, oysters (also for pearls), seaweed.

Farming inland

Aquaculture

Culture in fresh water (ponds, rivers) and brackish water (estuaries, lagoons). Sometimes with a rice crop.

Composite fish culture

5 or 6 species in one pond, each eating in a different zone, so they don't compete and all the food is used. Tap a fish.

Problem: many of these fish breed only in the monsoon, and wild seed gets mixed. Solution: breeding in ponds using hormonal stimulation gives pure fish seed.
15.2.4

Bee-keeping

Low investment, so farmers do it for extra income. Gives honey and wax (used in medicines). Bee farms are called apiaries.

Local varieties
  • Apis cerana indica · Indian bee
  • A. dorsata · rock bee
  • A. florae · little bee
Italian bee · A. mellifera
  • High honey collection
  • Stings less
  • Stays long in one hive
  • Breeds very well
Pasturage: the flowers available to bees for nectar and pollen. The amount decides how much honey; the kind of flowers decides its taste.
