Tissues in Action

A tissue is a group of cells, similar in structure, that work together to do a specific job. Tissues give us division of labour, which makes the body efficient.

Meristematic tissue: tissues for growth

Plants grow in length, in girth, and regrow after cutting or grazing. This needs actively dividing cells called meristematic tissue.

Features of meristem cells

  • Small cells with thin cell walls
  • Large, prominent nucleus
  • Dense cytoplasm, many organelles
  • Vacuoles absent
  • Tightly packed, little or no intercellular space

Annual growth rings

Rings in a cut tree trunk come from the lateral meristem. Count them to estimate the age of the tree. Wide rings = good years, narrow rings = poor growing years.

Differentiation: some new cells lose the ability to divide and become specialised for support, transport or storage. Meristematic tissue becomes permanent tissue by differentiation.
Meet a scientist: B. G. L. Swamy, Indian botanist known for plant morphology and anatomy. His Kannada book Hasuru Honnu won the Kendra Sahitya Akademi Award in 1978.
3.2.4

Permanent tissues

Simple = one type of cell. Complex = more than one type. Explore a T.S. of a sunflower stem: tap a layer.

Pick a tissue.

(i) Protective tissue · Epidermis

Structure

Single layer of tightly packed, flat, rectangular cells covered by waxy cuticle (made of cutin). Desert plants have a thick cuticle.

Root hair

Hair-like projections in roots that increase surface area to absorb water and minerals.

Stomata

Pores in leaf epidermis for gaseous exchange and transpiration. Transpiration creates a pull in xylem and removes wastes.

(iii)

Complex permanent tissues: xylem & phloem

Called complex because they are made of different types of cells working together.

Water & minerals · roots → up

Xylem

Also gives strength.

  • Tracheids and vessels: tubular, thick-walled
  • Xylem fibres
  • Xylem parenchyma: the only living part
Food · leaves → other parts

Phloem

Mostly living cells.

  • Sieve tubes: joined end to end by perforated walls, carry food
  • Companion cells: control loading and unloading of sugars
  • Phloem parenchyma: stores food, resin, tannins, latex
  • Phloem fibres: strength

Three tissue systems in plants

Dermal

Outer covering. Protects and reduces water loss.

Ground

Main body: parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma.

Vascular

Conducting tissues: xylem and phloem.

Ready to go beyond · Bark: in older stems, cells below the epidermis become cork cambium (a lateral meristem). It forms dead, compact cork cells that water and gases cannot pass through.

Part B · Animal tissues
3.3.1

Epithelial tissue

Covers the body (skin) and lines organs like mouth, lungs, blood vessels and intestine. Cells are closely packed: keeps germs out, reduces water loss, helps absorption and secretion.

3.3.2

Connective tissue

Connects and supports other tissues. Blood and bone are both connective tissues; they differ because of their matrix: watery in blood, hard in bone.

Blood

Plasma 55%
Formed elements 45%

RBCs

Red due to haemoglobin (iron-rich protein). Live about 4 months.

WBCs

Collect at infections: redness, swelling, pus.

Platelets

Help blood clot at a cut.

3.3.3

Muscular tissue

Voluntary movements are under our control (running, writing). Involuntary ones happen on their own (heartbeat, food moving in intestine).

Voluntary

Skeletal

  • Long, cylindrical, unbranched
  • Multinucleate
  • Striated (light and dark bands)
  • Attached to bones
Involuntary

Smooth

  • Spindle-shaped
  • Single nucleus
  • No striations
  • Stomach, intestines; slow movements
Involuntary

Cardiac

  • Cylindrical, branched
  • Single nucleus
  • Faint striations
  • Only in heart; works without fatigue

3.4–3.6

Musculoskeletal system, joints & skeleton

Bones, muscles, joints, cartilage, tendons and ligaments work together under the nervous system. Muscles pull bones through tendons. The skeleton is about 12–15% of adult body weight.

Vertebral column

Small bones called vertebrae, with cartilage discs between them for cushioning and flexibility. Protects the spinal cord.

Rib cage

12 pairs of ribs, joined to spine at back and sternum in front by flexible cartilage, so the chest can expand while breathing. Protects heart and lungs.

Think

Totipotency: one cell to a whole plant

In 1958, F. C. Steward grew a complete carrot plant from single phloem cells. The ability of a cell to form a whole organism is totipotency.

Cut 2 mg fragments of carrot root phloem
Culture in liquid nutrient medium with sugars and hormones
Stirring separates single cells
Cells dedifferentiate and divide into a mass of cells
They redifferentiate into root and shoot → plantlet → adult plant
Crown gall disease: tumour-like swellings caused by the bacterium Agrobacterium tumefaciens. Scientists now use it to put useful genes into plants for better crops.
